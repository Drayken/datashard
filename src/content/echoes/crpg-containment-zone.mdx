### Planescape: Torment: Enhanced Edition

Pretty much the entire reason I picked this game up was due to how often I've seen fans of the genre revere its writing as the best of any game ever. While I do have a few things to get into now that I've completed it, I reckon it's best to address that expectation immediately - it's not.

The plot and the extent of it are, for sure, commendable. However, it falls short in actually letting the player experience it in an interesting, interactive, and engaging way. I suspect part of this is due to the game having vastly fewer maps than other CRPGs of the same era _(like Baldur's Gate)_ - but much too often I lost interest clicking through blatant exposition dumps, like a developer unloading pages of internal wikipedia through NPCs, with quirks sprinkled in between in an attempt to make it feel like you're not just scrolling through infinite text files.

All in all, Planescape: Torment fails at one of, if not the most, important aspects of storytelling; **show**. **don't**. **tell**. What it did show? Decent enough. But why am I sitting here looking at 8 different dialog trees, each leading into a trillion paragraphs of detailed answers and backstories, from an NPC who 10 seconds ago claimed they'd knock my head off if I didn't leave them alone?

Beyond that, there's other problems that land it at a 5/10 - which to be fair is still perfectly average. The biggest one being that combat was very obviously an afterthought - mostly non-existent, then supplied in short bursts with super janky difficulty spikes you were never encouraged to prepare for, until you just... randomly wander into a 1-way cave entrance and end up shuffling back and forth between boring fights and the rest area to heal and get your spell casts back _(looking at you, Curst)_. You'll also routinely run into pathfinding issues - interacting with anything while running multiple party members leads to characters constantly getting stuck, doing nothing.

Honestly, it's hard to classify Planescape: Torment as a feature-complete CRPG, compared to others of the same era like Baldur's Gate, Icewind Dale, etc. In that way, it's very similar to Disco Elysium - albeit the latter mostly succeeded at not being a total fucking slog of constant exposition. Truth is, I just don't think Planescape: Torment's writing is that good when seen through the lens of a video game, or at least in the way in which it was executed. And that's coming from someone who was there for Myst, Riven, Syberia, etc.
